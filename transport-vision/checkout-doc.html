01 · Overview
What This Prototype Is

The Checkout prototype is a multi-tab exploration of the transport checkout flow — the moment in the auction buying experience where a dealer selects and confirms transport service for a vehicle they've just won. It is the buyer's first interaction with the transport product.

Five tabs explore different design directions and specific sub-problems: a faithful recreation of the current experience (reference baseline), a simplified redesign, a flat route layout, a fuel surcharge disclosure exploration, and a cost breakdown transparency exploration. Each tab is independently styled and scoped to avoid cross-contamination.

Navigation

A dark sidebar with an orange accent on the left provides tab navigation. Tab selection is persisted via localStorage so the active tab survives page refreshes — useful when testing or presenting a specific direction without having to re-navigate.

02 · Tab 01
Current — Reference Baseline

A faithful recreation of the existing ACV transport checkout experience. This tab is the reference baseline — it captures what dealers see today before any redesign, and grounds the other explorations in the current reality.

  • ACV-branded card with radio-based service selector: Open / Enclosed / Drive My Car.
  • Pickup and delivery address fields below the service selector.
  • Price estimates per service type.
  • "I'll find my own carrier" option collapses the ACV transport section.
  • .prod-rec-badge positioned inside .acv-card with position: relative on the card.
03 · Tab 02
Simplified — UX-Driven Redesign

A redesigned checkout that leads with the delivery address as the hero input — the most important piece of information for determining transport cost and availability. The service selector follows once the destination is established.

  • Delivery address as a prominent dropdown hero input at the top of the card.
  • Service selector below, with a benefits toggle per service type.
  • Estimates rendered below the service section.
  • "Own carrier" toggle collapses all ACV-managed sections, making it clear this is an opt-out of the default experience.
04 · Tab 03
Flat Pickup — Unified Route Block

Based on Tab 01, but replaces the separate pickup/delivery fields with a unified route block. Pickup location is shown as plain text (non-editable — it's the auction location). Delivery address is an underlined text input inline in the route block.

  • Route block as a single visual unit — origin (fixed) and destination (editable) side by side.
  • Estimates rendered at 20px using v2-estimate-value scoped class.
  • Reduces the form to its minimum — one field to fill, one decision to make (service type).
05 · Tab 04
Fuel Surcharge — Variable Cost Disclosure

Explores how a variable fuel surcharge (6.3%) is surfaced at checkout without obscuring the base price. The carrier market passes fuel cost volatility back to shippers — this tab tests a disclosure pattern that makes the surcharge transparent without making it feel punitive.

Key behaviors
  • Service rows show base prices with a "was" strikethrough (Open: $84 / $109, Enclosed: $279 / $359, Drive My Car: $75 flat).
  • The header price shows the total with surcharge included (base × 1.063). No strikethrough in the header — the total is the truth.
  • An orange info banner below the service selector shows the dollar surcharge amount (e.g. "+$5.29"). Updates dynamically when the service type changes.
  • Drive My Car is excluded from the surcharge — the banner does not update and the header stays at $75.
Banner styling
  • Orange background: #fef3ec
  • Info icon: #f26522
  • Surcharge amount: black (same weight as body copy)
  • Layout reuses .prod-info-box styling for consistency with other info messages in the checkout.
This exploration is documented in the ACV Transport Knowledge Base (Section 8.4). The surcharge amount and rate are illustrative — actual rates would come from a backend calculation at checkout time.
06 · Tab 05
Details — Cost Breakdown Transparency

Based on the Simplified tab. Explores what happens when selecting a service type reveals a detailed cost breakdown inline — showing the carrier payout, service charge, and what the service charge covers. Designed for buyers who want to understand what they're paying for before committing.

Inline expansion behavior
  • Selecting a service type expands that service card inline — the breakdown appears below a 2px #dcdfe8 divider.
  • Breakdown shows: Quote (orange, prominent), Estimated carrier/driver payout, Service Charge.
  • "Your service charge covers:" bullet list: extra arbitration days, carrier sourcing and negotiations, damage and fraud protection.
  • Drive My Car: uses "driver payout" label. "See benefits" link is hidden (benefits are not applicable to owner-provided transport).
Visual design
  • Font stack: DM Sans (body) + DM Mono (all prices and estimate values).
  • Background: #f4f4f2 warm off-white. Cards and service rows at 8px radius throughout.
  • Price header: 28px/500 DM Mono in orange — the dominant number on the card.
  • Service rows have a subtle scale(0.99) on :active for tactile feedback.
  • Delivery field focus state: background: #fff3ec on :focus-within — orange tint signals active input without a visible border.
CSS for this tab is fully scoped to #design-5 and #design-4 to prevent any styles from bleeding into other tabs. JS data uses d5ServiceData keyed by 'open' | 'enclosed' | 'dmc'.
07 · Decision Log
Key Design Decisions
DecisionRationale
Tab persistence via localStorage Designers and stakeholders frequently share links or refresh during review. Losing the active tab on refresh creates unnecessary friction when presenting a specific direction.
Each tab independently styled (scoped CSS) Tabs explore meaningfully different visual directions. Sharing a global stylesheet would force each tab to override the others, making the code fragile and changes risky. Scoped CSS keeps each tab self-contained.
Tab 01 as faithful reference, not idealized A redesign needs a true baseline, not a polished version of the current. Tab 01 captures the current experience as-is so comparisons are honest.
Fuel surcharge in header = total (no strikethrough) The header price is the price the dealer pays. Showing it with a strikethrough or "was" treatment would suggest the surcharge is a discount or error. The true total is the right thing to show prominently.
Drive My Car excluded from surcharge DMC uses a fixed platform fee — there is no carrier network or fuel cost variable involved. Applying the surcharge to DMC would be factually incorrect.
Tab 05: DM Mono for prices Monospace numerals align naturally in columnar layouts and feel more precise than proportional fonts for financial values. DM Mono pairs with DM Sans without feeling mismatched.
